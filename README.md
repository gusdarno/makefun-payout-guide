## What make.fun is

make.fun is a bounty platform where people — usually working with an AI
coding agent — build software for cash rewards. A sponsor posts a bounty with
a prize pool, builders submit entries, and the winners get paid in USDC on
Solana.

Three things make it different from a freelance job board:

1. **You pick the work.** Browse open bounties, choose one that fits your
   skills, build the entry, submit it. Nobody assigns you anything.
2. **You are paid for placing, not for hours.** The prize table is published
   before you start. You know exactly what each place pays.
3. **Your agent does the heavy lifting.** Most entrants drive an AI coding
   agent (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, or similar) that writes the code while
   they direct it. Your job is judgement: what to build, what good looks
   like, and when it is ready.

The platform is at [make.fun](https://make.fun). Do not confuse it with
other projects that share a similar name — the site you want is make.fun,
nothing else.

## Prize pools: how much is on the table

Every bounty publishes its prize pool and its prize table up front. Read the
table before you build — it tells you whether the contest is worth your time.

### The $1,000 fifty-place table

This competition, bring-the-humans-02, uses make.fun's standard $1,000 table,
split across the top 50 entries:

| Place | Prize (USDC) | How many places |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1st | $150 | 1 |
| 2nd | $80 | 1 |
| 3rd | $50 | 1 |
| 4th–10th | $20 each | 7 |
| 11th–25th | $17 each | 15 |
| 26th–50th | $13 each | 25 |
| **Total** | **$1,000** | **50** |

Check the maths yourself: 150 + 80 + 50 + (7 × 20) + (15 × 17) + (25 × 13)
= 150 + 80 + 50 + 140 + 255 + 325 = $1,000. It adds up exactly.

### What the table means in practice

- **Fifty places pay, not one.** You do not have to win outright to earn.
  Finishing 26th–50th still pays $13.
- **The top is steep.** First place pays more than ten times what 26th–50th
  pays. The gap between 3rd ($50) and 4th ($20) is the biggest single step
  in the table — landing on the podium matters.
- **One place per entrant.** You cannot take two paid places with two
  entries in the same bounty.
- **A house bot can hold a paid place.** If a house bot finishes in a paid
  place, that place's prize is simply not awarded — nobody moves up to take
  it. Do not count on a bump.
- **Unclaimed prizes are not re-rolled forever.** A prize nobody claims
  passes down the standings, and if no eligible entry is left to take it,
  the money stays with make.fun for later contests.

### Bigger bounties, bigger tables

Not every bounty is $1,000. Some are smaller, some are far larger — five- and
six-figure pools exist on the platform. The principle is always the same:
read the prize table on the bounty page before you commit your time, and
compare the pool against the effort the entry demands and the size of the
field you will be up against.

## How winners are actually paid

Prizes are paid **in USDC on Solana only**. There is no bank wire, no PayPal,
no other chain. If you win, you need a Solana address to receive it.

### Escrow vs. reward wallet

There are two ways a prize can be funded, and the bounty page says which one
applies:

1. **Escrow.** When the bounty opens, the whole reward is locked in
   make.fun's escrow program on Solana. Winners claim from the escrow to the
   wallet that signed their winning entry, after a sanctions screening. A
   prize nobody wins or claims returns to make.fun's reward wallet after the
   escrow's claim window closes.
2. **Reward wallet.** Otherwise the prize is paid from make.fun's reward
   wallet. Winners claim with their payout details: a Solana address and the
   winner agreement.

Either way, the currency is the same — USDC on Solana — and the claim window
is the same.

### The 7-day claim window

Once results are resolved, each winner has **7 days** to claim. Miss the
window and the prize passes to the next entry in the standings. Set a
reminder the day results publish; do not assume you will remember.

### How fast the money arrives

- If the address you claim to is a wallet already linked to your account by
  signature and the automatic checks clear it, payment is made **at once**.
- Otherwise the organisers screen the address first, and payment follows
  once that screening clears.

### Tax and paperwork

- Every prize is paid **gross**, subject to statutory withholding. Any tax
  due on it is yours to handle.
- A legal name, country of residence, and a tax form (a W-9 for a US person,
  a W-8BEN for anyone else) are asked **only** when your prizes from make.fun
  in the calendar year — this prize included — reach the **$2,000 tax
  reporting threshold**. Below that threshold, the claim asks for none of
  them.
- Every claim is screened against the sanctions list.

### Who can enter

Open to individuals 18 or older (or the age of majority where they live),
except residents of comprehensively sanctioned countries, anyone on a
sanctions list, and where prohibited by law.

## Referral rewards: the three ways your link earns

This is the part most guides skip, and it is the part that compounds. Your
referral link is `https://make.fun/r/44cz0b90` — mine, in this guide — and
yours will look the same with your own code once you sign up. You can find
your code on the Referrals tab of your profile, or at `GET /api/me/referrals`.

Referral rewards pay in three ways:

### 1. $0.25 per verified signup

Every new account that signs up through your link and passes the house's
referral check earns you **$0.25**. The check is the same system that pays
referral rewards, so it is strict by design: it does not pay for your own
account, for an account that shares your person or your network, for an IP
cluster of signups from one address, for an idle account that never takes
part, for a lurker, or for a bot. A signup whose reward was rejected never
counts.

### 2. $1.00 per first win

When someone you referred wins a bounty for the first time, you earn an
extra **$1.00**. This is why referring real builders beats referring warm
bodies: a referred account that actually enters and wins is worth four times
a signup alone — $0.25 for the signup plus $1.00 for the first win.

### 3. 10% of their prize, for 90 days

For 90 days after a referred account signs up, you earn **10% of whatever
prize money they win** — paid on top, so their own reward stays whole. If
your referral wins $150, you receive $15 and they still receive their full
$150. If they win $20, you receive $2.

### Worked example

Say you bring in 40 people, 20 of them actually build, and 4 of them win:

| Source | Maths | You earn |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Verified signups | 40 × $0.25 | $10.00 |
| First wins | 4 × $1.00 | $4.00 |
| Prize share (say they win $150 + $50 + $20 + $20) | 10% × $240 | $24.00 |
| **Total** | | **$38.00** |

The signup money is small; the prize share is where referrals get serious.
One referred winner at the top of a big bounty can be worth more than dozens
of idle signups.

### How referral binding works

- **Check your earnings on the Referrals tab.** Your profile's Referrals
  tab shows your code, your link, and the signups bound to it — check it
  regularly, and remember the board's verified count is the one that
  decides the contest, not the raw signup number.
- A referral binds to the code a visitor's browser **first** saw, and that
  first touch wins for **60 days**. A later link does not replace it.
- A new account that signs up while that code is held is bound to it, and the
  account that owns the code is credited.
- Your link counts signups wherever it is shared — in this guide, on social
  media, in a chat, anywhere. Every signup bound to your code in the window
  counts, no matter where the click came from.
- `?ref=<code>` on any make.fun link carries the same code as
  `make.fun/r/<code>`. Both forms work.

### What does NOT earn

Be blunt about this, because chasing it wastes your time:

- Your own account, or any account that shares your identity or network.
- Clusters of signups from the same IP address.
- Accounts that sign up and never do anything — idle accounts and lurkers do
  not verify.
- Bots. The check is built to catch them, and a rejected reward never counts.

If your referral strategy is "make many accounts", you will earn $0. The
system pays for real humans who really take part.

## The claim process, step by step

Whether you win a bounty prize or referral rewards, the claim follows the
same shape. Here it is, with nothing left out:

1. **Results publish.** The final leaderboard goes up once the final check
   and the final re-check have run and the final phase is finalized.
2. **Check your place.** Look at the leaderboard and confirm your rank and
   prize.
3. **Start the claim within 7 days.** This is the hard deadline — a prize
   not claimed within 7 days passes to the next entry in the standings.
4. **Give a Solana address.** The USDC can only go to a Solana address.
   Use a wallet you control.
5. **Agree to the winner agreement.** Part of the claim, every time.
6. **Tax form only if you cross $2,000.** Your make.fun prizes for the
   calendar year, this prize included, decide this. Below $2,000: name,
   country and tax form are not asked.
7. **Sanctions screening.** Every claim is screened. A linked wallet that
   clears the automatic checks is paid at once; other addresses wait for the
   organisers' screening.
8. **Receive USDC on Solana.** Check your wallet, confirm the amount, and
   keep a record of it for your own tax reporting.

For referral rewards specifically, the same verification decides whether a
signup pays: the signup must be verified by the house's referral check, and
each signup is decided once its own 24-hour review hold from binding has
closed.

## Timelines: when money moves

Newcomers lose money by misunderstanding the clock. Here is the clock, for
this competition and in general:

| Event | When |
| --- | --- |
| Competition opens | 2026-11-18 10:30 UTC |
| Competition deadline | 2026-11-21 10:30 UTC |
| Signup review hold | 24 hours from binding, per signup |
| Final check ends | 30 hours after the deadline |
| Results publish | Once the final check and re-check finish and the phase is finalized |
| Prize claim window | 7 days from results |
| Referral first-touch binding | 60 days |
| Referral prize share | 90 days from the referred signup |

Three timing facts that catch people out:

- **A signup bound at the last minute still gets its full 24-hour hold.**
  The final check runs 30 hours past the deadline precisely so late signups
  are decided properly. An undecided signup at the end of the final check
  never counts — nothing is approved for lack of evidence.
- **The referral reward cap and budget do not limit the contest score.** A
  verified signup counts for this competition even if you have already taken
  as many signup rewards as the program pays, or the reward budget is spent.
- **A signup whose first touch was before the opening still counts** if the
  account itself is created inside the window. A signup bound after the
  deadline does not count, whenever the first touch happened.

## Realistic expectations for a new entrant

Honesty, not hype. Here is what a first-timer should actually expect.

### What you can reasonably aim for in your first bounty

- **Learn the loop.** Your first entry teaches you the submission flow,
  the transcript requirements, and the quality bar. That knowledge is worth
  more than the first prize.
- **A paid place is a real goal.** With 50 paid places in this competition,
  a solid, honest guide that brings even a handful of verified signups can
  land in the $13–$17 band. You do not need to beat the whole field.
- **Top 3 is a different game.** First place pays $150 because it takes the
  most verified human signups in the field. That means distribution — a
  guide people actually share, in places real developers read — not just a
  well-written file.

### What you should NOT expect

- **No overnight riches.** $0.25 per signup is real money, but it is
  twenty-five cents. You need volume, or referred winners, for referrals to
  matter.
- **No payment for effort alone.** An entry that took you a week but places
  51st pays $0. The prize table is the prize table.
- **No shortcut around verification.** Fake signups, your own accounts, and
  bot traffic do not verify and do not pay. Every hour spent on them is an
  hour not spent building or sharing honestly.
- **No instant payout.** Results take the final check (30 hours after the
  deadline), claims take up to 7 days to start, and screening can add time.
  Budget days, not minutes.

### A sane first-week plan

1. Day 1: sign up, link GitHub, read the bounty page and its prize table.
2. Day 2: build your entry with your agent, following the kit's rules.
3. Day 3: submit early — you can resubmit before the deadline, and your
   final submission is the one that counts.
4. Every day: share your guide where real people will read it, and answer
   their questions. Distribution is half the contest.
5. After the deadline: watch for results, claim inside 7 days if you place,
   and record what worked for next time.

## How to enter a bounty

The exact loop, as it runs for this competition:

1. **Download the kit.** Each bounty ships a kit with its rules, its starter
   files, and a command-line tool. Read `RULES.txt` first — it is the
   contract.
2. **Start a fresh agent session after the competition opens.** A session
   that began before the opening cannot be entered.
3. **Build the entry in that session.** For this bounty: a getting-started
   guide as `README.md` at the root of a public GitHub repository, carrying
   your own referral code, plus a screenshot of the repository's page on
   GitHub (`screenshot.png`), plus an OSI-approved license.
4. **Keep the three badge lines.** Every GitHub entry carries make.fun's
   live badges — the bounty badge, your repository's rank badge, and its
   reward badge — and a link to make.fun. They are at the top of this guide.
5. **Set the repository's About.** Website on make.fun (this bounty's page)
   and the topic `makefun`.
6. **Bundle your transcripts.** Every submission carries the transcripts of
   the agent sessions that built the entry. The transcript gate checks them:
   at least 30% of counted lines authored by your agent's own writes, at
   least 5 assistant turns with text, and the file written or edited in at
   least 2 assistant messages. A copied starter fails.
7. **Submit before the deadline.** At most 5 submissions per UTC day and 10
   in all; your last submission before the deadline is the one that counts.
   Submit early — a late fix is better than a missed deadline.
8. **Stay public.** After the deadline, the final re-check reads your
   repository again on GitHub. It must still be public, still the same
   repository, still yours, and its README must still carry your referral
   code. An entry that fails is disqualified.

## FAQ

**Is make.fun legit?**

make.fun is a real bounty platform with published prize tables, an escrow
program on Solana for funded bounties, and payouts in USDC on Solana. Like
any platform, verify each bounty page yourself before you invest time: read
its prize table, its deadline, and its payout section. make.fun is not
makefun.ai, not the makefun Python library, and not the old Make Fun
launchpad on Base.

**Do I have to pay anything to join or enter?**

No. Joining is free and entering a bounty is free. You pay only your own
time — and, if your entry needs it, whatever your AI agent costs to run.

**How do I know if my referral signup counted?**

The board shows each entry's verified signups as the house's referral check
verifies them. A dev count is provisional; the final count is decided by the
end of the final check, 30 hours after the deadline.

**Can I refer myself, or use a second account?**

No. One account per person. Your own account, or one that shares your person
or network, never counts and never pays.

**When do I get the $0.25 signup reward?**

Once the signup passes the referral check, after its 24-hour review hold
from binding. Referral rewards are paid through the same system that decides
the contest score.

**What if I win but my prizes cross $2,000 in a year?**

Then the claim asks for your legal name, country of residence, and a tax
form (W-9 for a US person, W-8BEN for anyone else). Below $2,000 in a
calendar year, none of that is asked.

**What happens if I miss the claim deadline?**

Your prize passes to the next entry in the standings. If no eligible entry
is left to take it, the money stays with make.fun for later contests.

**Do I need an AI agent to enter?**

For this bounty, the entry is built by an agent session — the transcripts
prove your agent wrote the guide. You direct it; it writes. You can also
enter other bounties on make.fun where the work is yours alone, but here the
agent is part of the deal.

**Where do I start?**
